# Home contents inventory: user guide

## Who it is for

Anyone who keeps their phone photos and receipts on the NAS and would have to prove what they owned after a flood, fire or theft. Insurers ask for a list of every item with proof that you owned it and what it cost. Most people rebuild that list by hand, from memory, in the worst week of their year.

## What it does

- **Every week or so**, say "update my home inventory". It looks through the photos and receipts added since last time, finds the belongings in them, pairs each one with its receipt where there is one, and asks you to confirm. Then it puts the new photos in a dated Home contents album, tags every inventory photo and receipt with home-contents, and saves a dated list in your Home Contents folder.
- **After a loss**, say "I had a flood on June 20 in the basement, list what I lost". It finds, for each item in that room, a photo taken before that date, the receipt if you have one, the damage photos taken after, and camera events if it was a break-in. You confirm the list and fill in any values, and it saves a contents claim document and a claim album you can attach to your insurer's email.

## What it will not do

- It never estimates what something is worth. Values come from your receipts or from you.
- It never deletes, moves or overwrites a file, and asks before creating any album, tag, folder or file.
- It never sends anything off the NAS.
- If it cannot find proof for an item, it says so instead of using a similar photo.

## Things you can say

- "Update my home inventory."
- "File this month's receipts into my inventory."
- "What do I own in the living room?"
- "There was a break-in last night, prepare my contents claim."
- "Here is a voice note walking through the basement, list what was damaged on June 20."
